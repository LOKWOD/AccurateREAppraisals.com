SMALL INCOME PROPERTY

Two- to Four-Family Residential Appraisal Guide

Small residential income properties are bought for shelter and income. A credible appraisal should describe each unit accurately and connect actual operation with market rent, expenses and comparable investor behavior.

Updated August 17, 2026. Educational information only; not legal, tax, lending, engineering or accounting advice.

Verify unit configuration and use

Document kitchens, baths, bedrooms, entrances, parking, utilities and common areas. Public records may not match current occupancy.

Provide permits, certificates or zoning information when available. The appraiser is not the code authority but must analyze observed use and available legal-use evidence.

Collect complete income documents

Provide leases, rent roll, concessions, security arrangements, owner-paid utilities and vacancy history.

Distinguish family or below-market arrangements from arm's-length leases. The appraiser also researches market rent independently.

Inspect building systems and every unit

Unit condition may vary while roofs, porches, boilers, electrical and plumbing affect the entire building.

Inaccessible units can materially limit analysis. Coordinate tenants and access before the appointment.

Develop sales and income indications

Comparable sales reveal price behavior; income analysis considers market rent, vacancy, expenses and investor yield expectations.

Gross-rent multipliers are useful only when derived from genuinely comparable properties with similar utility and expense responsibilities.

Stabilize expenses and reconcile

Actual expenses should be organized, but one year's low maintenance or owner labor may not reflect typical operation.

The final reconciliation weighs data reliability, unit similarity, location, condition and the motivations of likely buyers.

Assignment planning checklist

  • Verify unit count
  • Provide access to each unit
  • Supply leases
  • Supply rent roll
  • Identify owner-paid utilities
  • Provide zoning documents
  • List capital improvements
  • Organize expenses
  • Explain concessions
  • Identify mixed-use areas

Frequently asked questions

Is a four-unit property commercial?

For many appraisal and lending contexts it remains residential, but the exact assignment and program control.

Are rents necessary?

Yes, when income is relevant; actual and market rent both inform analysis.

Can an empty unit be appraised?

Yes, with inspection and market evidence, though actual income and condition must be understood.

What if records show fewer units?

The conflict should be researched and disclosed rather than ignored.

Need a property-specific scope?

Send the address, intended use, effective date and material property characteristics before scheduling.
